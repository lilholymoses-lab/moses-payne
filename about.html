About the Author

I have been interested in the question of reality for as long as I can remember.

Growing up, I was fascinated by the human body. I found it incredible how complex it was, how many different parts were connected, and how everything seemed to work together as a whole. Eventually, my curiosity carried me beyond the human body and toward the stars. In a way, there was an irony in that journey: the deeper I looked into where I came from and how I was formed, the farther outward I found myself looking. What began as a fascination with the human body gradually became a desire to understand the stars, the cosmos, and the reality that gave rise to all of it. I started wondering how everything fit together—my own experience, the human body, science, space, the cosmos, and everything in between.

Before I had words like mathematical philosophy or scientific metaphysics to describe what I was doing, I was simply trying to figure out what reality was.

The question that has probably stayed with me the longest is a simple one:

Why does any of this exist at all?

The framework presented in these essays has been developing since I was in high school. In one sense, I feel like I have been developing it my entire life. Ideas I encountered along the way have been incorporated, changed, discarded, and rebuilt. But the specific framework you find here began taking shape during those earlier years and has gradually become more focused.

At one point, I was developing these ideas as an atheist. Today, I approach them as a theist. That is a significant change, but I don’t see the journey as abandoning the questions I started with. I see it as part of the development of the framework itself.

Over the last several months, I have devoted a great deal of time to bringing these ideas together into something coherent—to define the concepts, test the relationships between them, map them visually, and understand how the different pieces fit together.

That has not been easy.

One of the hardest parts has been making the metaphysical ideas logically coherent and finding ways to represent them clearly. Learning the mathematics behind some of the ideas has been another major challenge. The science has been challenging as well. There have been many difficult parts, but the work has always felt worth continuing.

I have never really wanted to stop.

There have been moments of exhaustion, because developing something like this takes an enormous amount of time and energy. But I never stopped wanting to understand it.

On a personal level, this project means something deeper to me than simply producing a book.

In some ways, it is a form of inner-child work.

These are things I have wanted to do since I was young. If I could speak to my younger self—the kid who was fascinated by the human body, who looked toward the stars, and who wanted to understand how everything fit together—I think he would be amazed that I eventually sat down and tried to pursue those questions seriously.

There is something meaningful to me about finally doing the things I once dreamed of doing.

But ultimately, I am not asking anyone to read this because I want them to think a certain way about me. What I hope they find here is a logical framework for considering the possibility that relationship is fundamental to reality.

We all experience relationship as something fundamental to our lives. We exist through relationships with other people, with our bodies, with our surroundings, and with the world we inhabit. The question I wanted to explore was what happens when that intuition is extended beyond our immediate experience and considered as a possible foundation for the workings of reality itself.

My hope is that these essays might give someone a different way of understanding both the universe and their own experience within it.

Perhaps, through that understanding, they may come to see relationship not merely as something that happens within reality, but as something fundamental to reality itself—and perhaps even discover that there is a deeper relationship to be found at that foundation.

If you have read this far, I genuinely appreciate it.

This project has taken years of thought and, especially recently, an enormous amount of time to bring into a coherent form. The fact that you chose to click on this work, spend time with it, and engage with these questions means more to me than I can easily express.

I don’t expect every reader to agree with me.

I don’t even expect every reader to come away with the same interpretation.

I simply hope the ideas give you something worth thinking about.

And if you make it all the way through the project, I would leave you with one question:

If relationship is the foundation of reality, what would change if you discovered that your relationship with the Source was not something you had to create, but something you were already part of—and were being invited to become more fully aware of?
