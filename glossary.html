Framework Glossary

Key terms in The Geometry of Becoming

This glossary establishes the meanings of key terms as they are used within The Geometry of Becoming. Some terms also have established meanings in science, mathematics, philosophy, or theology. Where a definition expresses this framework’s interpretation rather than an established disciplinary conclusion, that distinction should be kept in view. The glossary is intended to keep terminology consistent across Project One, the Bridge, and Project Two.

I. Foundations of Reality

Undivided Potential

The foundational condition of capacity and possibility considered prior to the expression of distinctions. Undivided Potential does not mean nothingness and does not describe a moment in time before the universe. It names ontological priority: reality considered before distinctions are expressed.

Unity

The wholeness or coherence within which differentiation can occur. Unity does not mean that everything is indistinguishable; it means that distinctions arise within an underlying whole rather than as absolute separation.

Differentiation

The emergence of distinction within unity. Differentiation allows what is held together within a whole to become distinct in modes, patterns, functions, forms, or directions without requiring absolute separation from the whole.

Possibility

The range of ways something could become under the conditions available to it. Possibility describes what may occur; by itself, it does not determine what will occur.

Potential

A capacity or disposition to become something under appropriate conditions. Potential concerns what a thing or system is capable of becoming, while possibility concerns the range of outcomes that could occur.

New Potential

A newly opened capacity or range of further development that emerges through becoming. New potential is not merely another possibility already available in the prior state; it is what the new organization makes possible next. For example, the becoming of structure opens potential for physical actuality, while the becoming of actuality opens potential for complexity.

Relational Field

The underlying relational ground within which differentiation, possibility, relationship, and becoming can occur. In this framework, the term refers to a proposed deeper relational condition and is not synonymous with a physical field such as a quantum field.

Divine Relational Field

The theological expression of relational ground within God. It refers to the eternal relational life of God without reducing the divine persons to an undifferentiated entity or treating this theological concept as a physical field.

II. The Generative Process

Relationship

A structured reciprocal dynamism through which distinct centers or aspects co-arise, influence one another, and participate in further becoming. Within this framework, relationship is not merely an interaction between already completed things; it can contribute to the formation of what relational participants become.

Relationality

The condition of existing or becoming through relation. Within this framework, relationality is not merely an optional property added to already independent things; it is part of the way things are constituted and develop.

Generative Relationship

A relationship understood as productive rather than merely connective. A generative relationship contributes to the emergence of new organization, a new physical expression, or further potential.

Becoming

The generative process through which organization develops and new forms or capacities emerge through relationship. Becoming is not simply any change; in this framework, it emphasizes the formation of new organization and the opening of further potential.

Generative

Having the capacity to produce new organization, a new expression, or further potential through relationship. A generative process does not merely preserve what already exists; it contributes to what can become.

Generative Movement

The movement through which reality is described as proceeding from unity through differentiation, possibility, relationship, and becoming, opening new potential for further organization. This is the framework’s account of a recurring generative logic, not a claim that every system follows an identical literal sequence.

Organization

An ordered arrangement of relations that gives a system a degree of stability, structure, or functional coherence. Organization can make patterns persistent and can establish conditions for further becoming.

Constraint

A condition that limits the range of possible outcomes while helping determine which developments are available or likely. Constraint does not merely restrict becoming; it can give becoming a particular form.

Emergence

The appearance of a new organization or property through relationships among components. Emergence does not necessarily mean creation from nothing; it describes how new forms or properties arise through organized interaction.

Persistence

The capacity of an organization or form to remain identifiable across change. Persistence does not require absolute immutability; a form may endure while its components or states change.

Co-arise

To emerge or develop together through relationship rather than entirely independently. What co-arises can remain distinct even when its development depends on shared relational conditions.

Co-inform

To influence one another’s state, organization, or development through relationship. Co-informing describes reciprocal influence; it does not by itself imply that the participants are identical or equally influential.

Co-transcend

To move beyond a previous state or organization through relationship, opening a form of becoming not available in the same way to the participants considered separately. This is a framework term for relational transformation, not a claim that every interaction produces transcendence.

III. The Levels of Organization

Structure

An organized configuration of relations that establishes conditions, constraints, and possibilities for what can become. Structure is the first major level in the framework and is not merely a passive container. It may be investigated through physical descriptions such as spacetime, fields, and geometry; the framework’s broader metaphysical interpretation should be distinguished from the scientific descriptions themselves.

Actuality

A general term for something being real or actual. Within this framework, the term has two senses that must be distinguished: ontological actuality and physical actuality. When discussing the transition from Structure to Actuality in the six-level sequence, the intended sense is physical actuality unless otherwise stated.

Ontological Actuality

The reality or existence of something considered without requiring it to be a tangible, localized, or physically definite object. This term distinguishes being real in a broad ontological sense from having a particular form of physical expression. It is a philosophical distinction used by the framework, not a separate experimentally established category in physics.

Physical Actuality

The definite physical expression of an organized condition as a distinguishable physical form or event. Physical actuality does not mean only something visible or touchable; it includes physical entities and processes that are not tangible in the everyday sense. In the six-level sequence, this is the specific sense of actuality associated with the transition from Structure to Actuality.

Ontological Delay

The distinction between an underlying or constitutive condition and its definite physical expression, where the difference is understood as a difference in level or mode of expression rather than necessarily as elapsed time. The term is used within this framework and should not be confused with a measured physical delay unless a specific scientific mechanism and evidence are supplied.

Complexity

A level of organization in which multiple actual forms enter into relationships and generate a larger organization with properties or possibilities not present in the same way at the prior level. Complexity is not simply a larger number of components; it concerns the organization of their relationships.

Reflexivity

A level or mode of organization in which processes act back upon the conditions or organization in which they occur. Reflexive organization can include feedback, regulation, and self-organization. Reflexivity alone does not establish that a system is alive or conscious.

Life

A level of organization in which interdependent processes maintain the continued viability of a living whole. Living systems regulate, repair, develop, and often reproduce through continuous interaction with their surroundings. The framework emphasizes self-maintenance as a distinguishing feature, while recognizing that biological definitions of life can vary by context.

Consciousness

A level of organization in which processes are available within experience. In the framework, consciousness involves experiencing or being aware of aspects of a system’s world and may include recognition, interpretation, reflection, and response. The definition does not assume that consciousness has been fully explained by describing information processing or observable behavior.

Becoming Actual

The transition in which an organized condition becomes definitely physically expressed as a distinguishable physical form or event. In this glossary, the term refers to physical actuality and does not imply that the prior condition was absolutely unreal.

Becoming Complex

The transition in which multiple actual forms enter into relationships that generate a larger organization with new properties or capacities.

Becoming Reflexive

The transition in which an organization begins to act back upon the conditions or processes that constitute it, allowing feedback or regulation to affect its own activity.

Becoming Life

The transition in which reflexive and interdependent processes become organized around maintaining the viability and continued activity of a living whole.

Becoming Conscious

The transition in which organized processes become available within experience, allowing aspects of the world or of the system’s own activity to be experienced, recognized, interpreted, or responded to.

IV. Patterns and Transitions

Microcosm

The recurring generative logic expressed within a particular level or scale of organization. A microcosm is not a miniature copy in which every detail of the whole is repeated; it is a local expression of the same proposed pattern of unity, differentiation, possibility, relationship, and becoming.

The Five-Part Pattern

The recurring sequence used to describe the generative movement: Unity → Differentiation → Possibility → Relationship → Becoming. The sequence names conceptual functions within the framework; it should not automatically be read as a literal chronological sequence in every physical system.

The Six Levels

The six major organizational expressions in the framework: Structure → Actuality → Complexity → Reflexivity → Life → Consciousness. Each level is proposed to express the generative pattern in a different organizational form. The sequence is the framework’s conceptual map and should not be mistaken for a complete scientific taxonomy or a claim that every system passes through every level.

The Generative Sequence

The larger movement described as Unity → Differentiation → Possibility → Relationship → Becoming → New Potential → further becoming. New potential names the opening created by becoming that allows further development or organization.

Inside

The perspective that considers reality in terms of generative relational process: how becoming unfolds through relationships and organizing conditions. ‘Inside’ names a mode of inquiry, not a claim that reality is literally enclosed within a separate inner realm.

Outside

The perspective that considers reality in terms of organized expression, including observable structures, forms, fields, interactions, and organizations. ‘Outside’ names a mode of inquiry, not a separate reality independent of the inside perspective.

Bridge

The conceptual movement connecting the inside and outside perspectives. The Bridge explores how generative relational process and organized physical expression may be interpreted as two standpoints on the same becoming, while keeping metaphysical interpretation distinct from scientific evidence.

Identity Test

A method for examining whether a relationship is constitutive of an organization. If removing or changing the relationship would fundamentally alter the organization or what makes it the kind of thing it is, that is evidence that the relationship may be constitutive rather than incidental. The result depends on how the system and the proposed intervention are defined.

Relational Structure

An organization whose form or behavior depends constitutively on relationships among its parts or distinctions. Relational structure emphasizes that organization cannot be adequately described solely by listing components apart from the relations that organize them.

V. Mathematical and Theological Interpretations

Purpose

Within the theological dimension of the framework, purpose is interpreted as movement toward fuller participation in relationship and becoming rather than merely the achievement of an externally imposed endpoint. This is a philosophical and theological interpretation, not by itself an empirically established scientific conclusion.

Kenosis

A Christian theological term meaning self-emptying. Within this framework, kenosis is interpreted in terms of voluntary self-limitation or the assumption of constraint without the loss of identity, making genuine participation in relationship possible. The framework’s application should be distinguished from the full range of meanings of kenosis in Christian theology.

Perichoresis

A Christian theological concept associated with mutual indwelling and participation without the collapse of distinction. Within this framework, it provides a theological parallel for distinction without separation; the parallel is interpretive and does not make the theological concept identical to a mathematical or physical structure.

Distinction Without Separation

A condition in which distinct persons, aspects, or forms remain genuinely distinct while also belonging to or participating in a relational whole. Difference does not require isolation, and unity does not require the elimination of difference.

Torus

A mathematical surface commonly represented as a doughnut-shaped surface. Within the framework, the torus is also used as a geometric analogy for continuous relational circulation, return, and further becoming. Its mathematical properties should be distinguished from the symbolic meaning assigned to it in the framework.

Infinity

In mathematics, infinity has precise meanings that depend on the mathematical context. Within the framework’s philosophical usage, infinity can also refer to inexhaustibility: that which cannot be exhausted by any finite expression or completed by a final finite state. These mathematical and interpretive uses should not be conflated.

New Potential After Finitude

A theological interpretation of renewed becoming after a finite state or separation. Within the framework, it describes the possibility of renewed participation in relationship without requiring the elimination of distinction. It is a specialized theological application of new potential, not a replacement for the general definition.

Relationship with the Source

The conscious participation of a distinct being in the originating reality from which it arises and through which its existence is sustained. Within the theological framework, this describes communion between the being and its ultimate source without the elimination of distinction.
